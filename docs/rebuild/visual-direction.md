# Visual direction correction — October 6

## Reference and honest assessment

Owner reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=747ZnEtsRbg — Lukas Margerie's motion-graphics workflow. Reviewed its description, chapter structure and selected reference-workflow frames, not every minute of the video. It emphasizes real brand assets, motion rules, reference-led direction and repeated critique. This is a useful production method; its promotional model/provider claims are not prerequisites for this project.

The current academy website is functional but does not yet meet the owner's cinematic standard. Several effects were added independently, so the page still reads as stacked sections rather than one deliberate composition. The dark editorial homepage and white customer screens have different visual languages. The court explorer is useful as a schematic, but its model presentation does not establish a strong brand image. Repeated photos and similarly paced content blocks weaken the visual hierarchy.

## Immediate correction

The 540×304 attachment should never have been presented as a large desktop photograph. Customer pages now use the actual 1595×900 academy night photograph, with allowed image quality 90 and a wide homepage frame. The attachment stays archived; it is not enlarged, regenerated or passed off as a higher-detail original. This fixes the avoidable source-resolution problem, not the entire design gap.

## Coherent next direction

Retain court blue, dark ink, restrained lime accents and athletic typography. Build the main composition around a single branded court/ball sequence, consistent customer-page typography and controls, and real venue photography used selectively. Keep availability and booking prominent throughout.

The proposed eight-second film should have three connected shots: a detailed ball under floodlights, a low camera pass over a blue court/net, then a clean Doon brand reveal. Design camera framing, materials, light, text hierarchy and the transitions together before adding motion. Preview and critique the actual composition at desktop and phone sizes before website integration. Keep its book action as accessible HTML, with pause/reduced-motion behavior.

Use the existing interactive explorer as a secondary utility rather than allowing a schematic to carry the whole brand. Remove repetitive photo treatments when the film is introduced. Judge the result by composition, asset fidelity, legibility and rhythm—not the number of animated effects.

The photo-quality correction initially delivered no film. The subsequent owner-authorized film pass implements this direction in `motion/court-film.tsx`: a perforated ball with moulded rims, a court-level camera pass over net mesh, then a Doon reveal. The eight-second 1280×720 H.264 output and matching poster are rendered locally. This is an authored 3D illustration, not footage or a surveyed model of the physical academy. No paid tools, audio services or cloud render are used.

The website uses a native muted video with explicit play/pause, no pre-hydration autoplay, reduced-motion/save-data gates, visibility pause and an honest poster fallback. The interactive court schematic is secondary and only loads on request. The homepage uses separate reverse-daylight and evening photographs; Contact, membership, booking review and sign-in have deliberate photo roles. This pass does not claim every customer screen has been redesigned; payment readiness remains unchanged. Verification and deployment evidence are recorded in `progress.md`.
